Hey kids! Let's talk about what MPs (Members of Parliament) do in Malaysia in a super simple way. Imagine the government is like a big team running the country, and everyone has their own job so no one has too much power.
The Cabinet (like the Prime Minister and ministers) is the team that plans and writes most new rules (called laws or bills). But they can't make them real on their own. That's where Parliament comes in – it's like the big meeting room where everyone checks and approves those plans.

MPs are the people we vote for to represent us in the lower house called Dewan Rakyat (there are 222 of them!). Their jobs are like this:

Speak for you and your area: MPs are like your voice in the big meeting. If there's a problem in your town (like bad roads or needing more schools), your MP tells everyone in Parliament about it.
Discuss and improve laws: The Cabinet sends new law ideas to Parliament. MPs talk about them a lot (this is called debating – like arguing but nicely to make things better!). They can suggest changes to make the law fairer.

Vote yes or no: After talking, MPs vote to say if the law is good or not. If most say yes, it becomes a real law!
Watch the government: MPs ask tough questions to the ministers, like "Why did you spend money this way?" or "Is this plan working?" This makes sure the government is doing a good job.
Approve money stuff: The government needs Parliament's okay to spend our country's money (like on hospitals, roads, or teachers' salaries).

So, even though the Cabinet makes most law ideas, MPs are super important because they make sure the laws are good for everyone – and they represent YOU! That's why voting for a good MP matters when you're older. Cool, right? 😊
